One of the most common questions new sellers ask is: *how do I know if my price is right?* Price too high and your listing sits forever. Price too low and you leave money on the table. Here's the framework we use ourselves.

## Step 1: Identify What You Have

Before pricing anything, nail down the exact card:

- **Set name and set code** (e.g., Base Set — BS)
- **Card number** (e.g., 4/102 for Charizard)
- **Edition** (1st Edition, Shadowless, Unlimited) — this matters enormously for vintage cards
- **Language** — English, Japanese, and other languages each have separate markets
- **Variant** — Holo vs. non-holo, reverse holo, full-art, promo

Our marketplace syncs with the Pokémon TCG database so entering the card name autofills most of these details.

## Step 2: Check Recent Sales (Not Listings)

This is the critical distinction. **Listed prices are wishes. Sold prices are facts.**

Check recent completed sales on major marketplaces. Look for:
- Same card name, same edition, same language
- Same or comparable condition

Filter for the last 30–90 days. Older sales may not reflect current demand.

## Step 3: Factor in Condition

Use this rough multiplier table relative to Near Mint pricing:

| Condition | Multiplier |
|-----------|------------|
| Mint (MT) | 1.1–1.3× NM |
| Near Mint (NM) | 1.0× (baseline) |
| Lightly Played (LP) | 0.7–0.85× NM |
| Moderately Played (MP) | 0.4–0.6× NM |
| Heavily Played (HP) | 0.2–0.35× NM |

These are approximations. Very popular cards (Base Charizard, Black Star promos) may command closer to NM pricing even in LP, while commons lose value quickly with wear.

## Step 4: Set Your Starting Price

With your market data in hand:

1. **Take the median** of your comparable sales — not the highest, not the lowest.
2. **Subtract your expected costs**: 6% commission + Stripe fee + shipping you absorb.
3. **Add a small premium** if your listing has an advantage (professional photos, detailed description, quick shipping).

If you need the card gone quickly, price 5–10% below median. If you can wait, price at or slightly above median.

## Step 5: Watch and Adjust

Don't just set it and forget it. Check back after 2 weeks:
- If no views, your price is likely too high or the card needs better photos.
- If views but no sales, consider a small price reduction (5–10%).
- If sold quickly, note the price — you may have been able to get more.

## Special Situations

### First Edition and Shadowless Cards
These carry massive premiums over Unlimited prints. Base Set 1st Edition cards can be 10–30× the Unlimited price for the same condition. Always verify edition before pricing.

### Sealed Packs and Boxes
Never opened product prices are volatile and speculation-driven. Research specifically — prices can swing 20–30% in weeks around set releases and anniversaries.

### Bulk Commons and Uncommons
Price these in lots, not individually. A bulk lot of 100 random Commons might sell for CHF 5–10 total. Sorting and individually listing low-value cards is usually not worth the time.

Price confidently, price fairly, and you'll build the seller reputation that keeps buyers coming back.

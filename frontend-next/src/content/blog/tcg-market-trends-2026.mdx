The Pokémon TCG market never stands still. After the explosive 2020–2021 boom and the subsequent correction, the market has stabilised into a more mature phase — but several forces are reshaping where the value is. Here's what we're watching in 2026.

## 1. Japanese Cards Continue to Outperform

Japanese Pokémon cards have quietly become one of the best-performing segments of the collectables market. Several factors drive this:

- **Exclusive artwork variants** that never see English release
- **Smaller print runs** relative to English sets
- **Growing international collector base** accessing the Japanese market for the first time through online platforms

The most in-demand cards — Pikachu illustrator promos, Art Rares, and Special Art Rares from recent sets — regularly trade at 2–4× their English equivalents when available.

**Trend watch:** Look for cards with exclusive "tera" or full-bleed illustration variants that won't be reprinted in English.

## 2. Graded Cards Are Stabilising

The grading boom of 2020–2022 flooded the market with PSA and BGS slabs, pushing down premiums for common vintage cards in high grades. In 2026:

- **PSA 10s of truly rare cards** (Skyridge Charizard, Gold Star Umbreon) remain strong
- **PSA 8–9s of common vintage** are trading near raw NM pricing
- Grading is now primarily worth it for cards valued above ~CHF 500 raw

For everyday sellers: raw condition cards are fine and don't require professional grading to sell.

## 3. The Sealed Product Market is Maturing

Sealed booster boxes and ETBs (Elite Trainer Boxes) from recent sets are no longer guaranteed investments. Key observations:

- **Reprint risk is real** — popular sets get additional print runs that suppress prices
- **Vintage sealed (pre-2010)** remains a strong store of value, but liquidity is limited
- **Competitive sets** (sets with strong playable cards) hold value better than "collector" sets

If you're holding sealed product purely as an investment, diversify your thesis and have a clear exit strategy.

## 4. Grail Card Liquidity is Improving

Historically, ultra-high-end cards (Base Set 1st Edition Charizard PSA 10, Pikachu Illustrator PSA 10) rarely traded — there simply weren't enough buyers and sellers in the same place at the same time. This is changing:

- Dedicated high-end auction houses now hold quarterly sales
- Cross-border transactions are smoother with better payment infrastructure
- A new generation of collectors with higher budgets has entered the market

If you have a grail card, 2026 is arguably the best liquidity environment ever.

## 5. Bulk is Back

The "anything might be worth something" mentality that drove bulk prices to record highs has corrected. Commons and uncommons are back to reasonable levels, but:

- **Bulk from popular sets** (Celebrations, Base Set) still commands premiums
- **Reverse holos** from competitive sets are consistently undervalued and worth sorting
- **"Secret" bulk lots** with potentially high-value cards have become a collector hobby unto themselves

## What This Means for Sellers

1. **Photo your cards.** In an uncertain market, buyers are more discerning.
2. **Price relative to recent sales.** List prices are more disconnected from reality than ever.
3. **Don't bulk-sell rare Japanese cards without checking values** — even cards you don't recognise may be worth real money.
4. **Move quick on trending cards.** Market movements after set reveals can be fast. If you're holding a card that spikes, consider selling into the excitement.

## What This Means for Buyers

1. **Vintage raw cards offer value** for personal collections — graded premiums have compressed.
2. **Don't overpay for sealed product** unless you genuinely want to open it.
3. **Japanese cards are worth learning about.** The barrier to entry is lower than it seems.

The TCG market rewards knowledge and patience. Stay informed, grade honestly, and price based on data rather than hope.
